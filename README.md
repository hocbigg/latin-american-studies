# Summary

The Latin American Studies curriculum is a **complete education in Latin American Studies** using online materials.

The interdisciplinary nature of the field draws from history, political science, anthropology, literature, economics, geography, and cultural studies, emphasizing the region's diversity, colonial legacies, independence movements, modernization, and contemporary issues.

Language proficiency (Spanish or Portuguese) is integrated throughout, as it is essential for primary sources and deeper engagement. Aim for intermediate reading/speaking proficiency by the end of the Core Phase.

Note: When there are courses or books that don't fit into the curriculum but are otherwise of high quality,
they belong in [extras/courses](extras/courses.md), [extras/readings](extras/readings.md) or [extras/other_curricula](extras/other_curricula.md).

**Process**. Students can work through the curriculum alone or in groups, in order or out of order.

- We recommend doing all courses in **Core**, only skipping a course when you are certain that you've already learned the material previously.
- For simplicity, we recommend working through courses (especially Core) in order from top to bottom. Some students choose to study multiple courses at a time in order to vary the material they are working on in a day/week.
- The courses in the Advanced section are electives. Choose one track to specialize in and complete all the courses listed under it.

# Curriculum

## Intro Latin American Studies

| Subject | Why study? | Book | Online Course |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Geography and Environment of Latin America | Provides spatial context for historical, economic, and cultural patterns, including physical features, climates, and human-environment interactions. | [*Latin America: Regions and People* by Robert B. Kent](https://www.amazon.com/Latin-America-Regions-Guilford-Human/dp/1462525504) | Covered in Introduction to Latin American Studies (MIT OCW) |
| Introduction to Latin American Studies | Offers an interdisciplinary survey of the region's societies, cultures, economies, and global connections. | [*Latin America and the Caribbean: A Systematic and Regional Survey* by Brian W. Blouet and Olwyn M. Blouet](https://www.amazon.com/Latin-America-Caribbean-Systematic-Survey/dp/0470387734) | [Introduction to Latin American Studies](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/17-55j-introduction-to-latin-american-studies-fall-2006/) (MIT OpenCourseWare) |
| Pre-Columbian and Colonial History | Establishes foundations of indigenous civilizations, conquest, and colonial structures that shape modern Latin America. | [*Born in Blood and Fire: A Concise History of Latin America* by John Charles Chasteen](https://www.amazon.com/Born-Blood-Fire-History-America/dp/0393283054) | Covered in Modern Latin America, 1808-Present (MIT OCW) |
| Elementary Language Proficiency (Spanish or Portuguese) | Essential for accessing primary sources, literature, and cultural materials; no prerequisites. | Standard textbook such as *Ponto de Encontro* (for Portuguese) or *¡Hola, amigos!* (for Spanish); focus on grammar and basic reading. | [Basic Spanish](https://www.edx.org/learn/spanish) or free university YouTube playlists (various channels) |

## Core Latin American Studies

| Subject | Why study? | Book | Online Course |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Modern Latin American History (Independence to Present) | Covers independence movements, nation-building, revolutions, and 20th-century developments; central to understanding political and social evolution. | [*Born in Blood and Fire: A Concise History of Latin America* by John Charles Chasteen](https://www.amazon.com/Born-Blood-Fire-History-America/dp/0393283054) | [Modern Latin America, 1808-Present: Revolution, Dictatorship, Democracy](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21h-802-modern-latin-america-1808-present-revolution-dictatorship-democracy-spring-2005/) (MIT OpenCourseWare) |
| Latin American Politics and Government | Examines political systems, democratization, authoritarianism, revolutions, and U.S. relations; key for analyzing power dynamics. | [*Latin American Politics and Development* edited by Harvey F. Kline and Christine J. Wade](https://www.amazon.com/Latin-American-Politics-Development-Harvey/dp/1032121564) | Covered in Introduction to Latin American Studies (MIT OCW) |
| Latin American Economy and Development | Explores economic structures, dependency, inequality, neoliberalism, and growth models; explains persistent challenges like poverty and resource extraction. | [*The Economic History of Latin America since Independence* by Victor Bulmer-Thomas](https://www.amazon.com/Economic-History-America-since-Independence/dp/1107608554) |  |
| Latin American Literature and Culture | Introduces major literary movements (e.g., Boom, Magical Realism) and cultural expressions; reveals identity, social critique, and aesthetics. | [*The Cambridge Companion to the Latin American Novel* edited by Efraín Kristal](https://www.amazon.com/Cambridge-Companion-Latin-American-Novel/dp/0521825334) | [Latin American Culture](https://www.coursera.org/learn/latinamericanculture) (Tecnológico de Monterrey via Coursera - audit free) |
| Anthropology and Society in Latin America | Addresses indigenous peoples, ethnicity, gender, social movements, and urbanization; provides sociocultural depth. | [*The Anthropology of Latin America and the Caribbean* by Harry Sanabria](https://www.amazon.com/Anthropology-Latin-America-Caribbean/dp/0205380999) | Covered in Latin American Culture (Coursera) |
| Intermediate/Advanced Language Proficiency | Builds reading comprehension for primary texts, literature, and academic sources. | Advanced grammar/reader such as *Vistas* (Spanish) or continuation of elementary text | Continuation of elementary resources; university YouTube series |
